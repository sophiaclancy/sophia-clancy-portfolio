# Portfolio Audit: Bao To, Rachel Chen, Michelle Liu vs. Sophia Clancy
*Nov 18, 2026. Pages were measured in Chrome at 1440px. Grades are judgment calls based on those measurements and a read of the copy.*

**Pages studied**
- **Rachel (rachelchen.tech):** Figma, Notion, 1Password, OpenAI, Etch, Alexa, PokerGPT, Earth, home
- **Bao (baothiento.com):** Zen Editor, NotetakingLM, work, about, home
- **Michelle (liumichelle.com):** NASA, Apple, Roblox, Adobe, design system, about, home

**Limits:** Michelle's Apple, Roblox, and Adobe studies are partly password protected, so NASA is the main evidence for her long-form work. Phone layouts were not audited.

## 1. What makes each one work

### Rachel Chen: the clearest model for your case studies
- **One column.** Text and images share one 688px column. Only the hero breaks out, at 923px.
- **Very few type styles.** Essentially 36 / 28 / 18 / 15px, all at regular weight. Hierarchy comes from size and space.
- **A repeating unit.** Small label, 28px sentence-style heading, one short paragraph, one visual.
- **No boxes.** 0 boxed text cards across Figma, Notion, 1Password, and OpenAI.
- **Headings carry the story.** Read alone, they tell it: "Notion's Custom Agents are powerful..." then "...but with that power comes a setup problem."
- **She shows the road not taken.** "First pass," "Exploration 1," and "This just wasn't possible with Apple's API limitations," each with a visual, then "Solution:".
- **Plain, human voice.** "My mission was to bring clarity to this mess." "We won the SVSD design competition!"
- **Ownership is explicit and early.** "So my role was to bring setup precision to the chat!"
- **Short reflection.** Two takeaways, side by side.

### Bao To: the clearest model for product thinking
- **The hero asks the question.** "What if you could edit Google Docs like you edit code in Cursor?"
- **A standard skeleton.** Label, 30px heading, content, with about 192px between sections.
- **Short, rhythmic problem writing.** "Open ChatGPT. Copy your text. Paste it in."
- **The interface explains itself.** Zen Editor uses live demos: granularity switch, accept/reject, timing comparison.
- **Technical depth is concrete.** Five diff strategies with real timings (3–5ms, 10–20ms).
- **The ending invites use.** "See it yourself," with a link to the live product.
- **Watch out:** his text runs about 1168px wide (roughly 140 characters per line). Do not copy that.

### Michelle Liu: the clearest model for personality and credibility
- **A strong identity line.** "Designing tools for human connection & creativity."
- **Compact facts first.** Year, role, team, and collaborators, then "The Mission."
- **Two-column reading.** A short heading on the left, the explanation on the right.
- **Other people vouch for her.** Manager and client quotes are a real section.
- **Warmth is part of the craft.** "Daring (& designing) mighty things," "With ❤️, Michelle."
- **Numbers as a moment.** NASA's "By the numbers": 131 cards, 5 environments, 6 months.

## 3. Grades

**Overall: B-.** The thinking and prototypes are strong. The presentation makes them harder to feel.

| Category | Grade | Why |
|---|:---:|---|
| Problem framing and reframes | **A-** | "The brief asked for a game. Research asked for a starting point." is on par with the references. |
| Prototypes and interaction | **A-** | Your live demos are your biggest advantage. Only Bao's Zen Editor is comparable. |
| Storytelling | **B** | Strong openings, but the middle turns into categorized cards instead of a chain of events. |
| Decision making shown | **B-** | Rejected directions are described in text. Rachel shows them. |
| Technical credibility | **B** | Good ideas, but compressed. Bao gives specifics and numbers. |
| Voice | **B-** | Clever and clean, but many lines sound like slogans. Fewer sound like a person. |
| Outcomes and proof | **C+** | Contexto v2 is untested, Baro has no outcome, and no one vouches for your work. |
| Hero sections | **B-** | Clear, but the 56px title and status pill compete with the work. |
| Visual hierarchy | **C+** | 15–20 font sizes flatten the page. Everything asks for attention. |
| Breathability | **C+** | Cards fill the space that should separate ideas. |
| Layout consistency | **C** | Content jumps between 415, 680, and 1066px widths, with several card grids. |

## 4. What to fix, in order
1. **Adopt one type scale.** Four or five sizes and two weights across every case study.
2. **Use one reading column** of about 680–720px. Let only heroes and key media go wider.
3. **Remove most cards.** Turn them into sentences, simple lists, or visuals. Keep boxes for real interactive demos.
4. **Remove the colored stat boxes and tinted panels.** Use plain numbers on the page.
5. **Show rejected directions visually,** starting with Contexto's six versions.
6. **Rewrite section headings so they read as one story** when skimmed alone.
7. **Make the voice more human.** Say what happened, what went wrong, and what you decided.
8. **State your ownership in the first screen** of every case study.
9. **Add proof:** test results, launch status, or a quote from a teammate, client, or founder.
10. **Shorten Contexto** to about 1,100–1,200 words.
11. **Quiet the hero:** smaller title, fewer elements, work first.
