ASUS ProArt × Berkeley Innovation · In progress

Designing how ProArt creators work alongside AI agents.

ASUS is bringing agentic AI into ProArt’s creative tools. Our team is designing the interface between early-career creators and those agents: how a creator hands off work, stays in control, and keeps building their own judgment.

The challenge

Agents remove friction. Early-career creators need some of it to grow.

An agent doesn’t just answer questions. It takes a goal and carries out the steps: opening tools, moving files, making judgment calls. That’s powerful for production work, and risky for creators still learning what good looks like.

The creator is no longer prompting a tool. They’re delegating to one, and no interface pattern exists yet for handing creative work off and taking it back. Early-career creators build taste through exactly the decisions an agent would skip.

The question

Remove all friction, and you remove judgment.

How might we keep the productive friction that builds a creator’s judgment, when the whole point of an AI agent is to take friction away?

Where we’re headed

An interface where the creator sets the agent’s role.

Research pointed us away from a smarter agent and toward a clearer relationship with one.

The creator decides

How involved the agent is, task by task: do it, guide me, or hands off.

Advise by default

The agent consults and flags trade-offs. It builds only when asked.

Intent stays visible

The creator’s goal and style travel with the project.

My role

I lead the project, from scope to direction.

I guide the research, synthesis, ideation, and prototyping leads, pick the opportunity we focus on, run meetings to keep ASUS aligned, and ask the questions that narrow the brief.

So far

What I’m learning by leading it.

  1. The brief narrows through questions. My job is less about having answers and more about asking the ones that cut scope.
  2. Not all friction is bad. The same steps an agent would skip are where early-career creators build taste.
  3. Early signals aren’t findings. Twenty coded interviews point a direction; they don’t prove a pattern, so we say that out loud.
